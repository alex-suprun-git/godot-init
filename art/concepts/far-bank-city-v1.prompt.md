## Prompt

Use case: stylized-concept / precise-object-edit.
Asset type: exploratory visual direction for the far-bank city of a fictional brotherly post-Soviet republic, for a 1997 isometric strategy game.
Input image 1 is the existing BORDER TRANSPORT DISTRICT concept. Use it as the edit target and authoritative reference for camera, river geography, two bridge placements, near-bank layout and painterly game style.
Primary request: transform the sparse FAR / UPPER-RIGHT BANK into a believable, inhabited but relatively compact riverside town with its own architectural identity, while preserving the near / lower-left bank and the existing river and crossings. A highly polished wide landscape concept, not a schematic. The far-bank town is the main new visual subject and should be sufficiently visible to judge its architecture. Retain the full original framing and the important near-bank context; do not zoom into a single house.
KEEP: same elevated orthographic isometric camera and parallel architectural edges; broad diagonal river; exactly TWO separate crossings, a concrete road bridge upstream at upper-middle-left and a steel railway bridge downstream at right; working near-bank port between them, near-bank panel apartment blocks, bus/truck yards, village and pond. Roads and rails must continue coherently across their existing bridges onto the far bank. Water stays below bridge decks.
CHANGE ONLY THE FAR BANK: give it a more organically grown, older small-city fabric compared with our late-Soviet transport district. Mostly two- and three-storey masonry houses, restrained warm pale plaster and dark red/brown brick, weathered terracotta pitched roofs with chimneys, narrow connected streets and small enclosed backyards. These are modest Slavic provincial merchant-town buildings, not an ornate Central European fantasy or Mediterranean resort. Behind them, a few ordinary late-Soviet apartment blocks establish a shared past. Frame a small inhabited riverside promenade and a compact market square with a few muted fabric awnings. Include a small river landing with a simple passenger shelter, a modest brick municipal building with a recognisable low square clock tower, and one house with limited repair scaffolding. These give the player landmarks to recognise in future time variants. Keep market, landing and repaired house readable but modest. Small gardens, patches of worn paving and ordinary greenery; sparse 1990s pedestrians, Ladas, Volgas and older utility vehicles. On the far bank, the road bridge must feed into a practical checkpoint/approach and continue to the town streets; the railway bridge continues through a green rail corridor, not through houses. Keep a green bank strip so not every metre is built up; no packed megacity.
Lighting: match the reference's warm late-August 1997 afternoon and mostly green foliage, consistent sun and soft contact shadows. Both banks share the same art quality, season and weather. Town identity comes from building silhouettes, roofs, street grain and waterfront use, not a crude rich-versus-poor or warm-versus-cold colour division.
Style: detailed hand-painted textures on believable dimensional game architecture, rich yet readable at strategic zoom, grounded living city, coherent scale with reference vehicles and people. This is an art-direction proposal, not final implementation geometry.
Avoid: war, fire, ruins, tanks, barricades, military spectacle, modern skyscrapers or 2020s cars, real flags or actual country names, new bridges, duplicated bridge spans, giant palaces, exaggerated monuments, diagram blocks, UI, readable text, labels, collage, split panels, borders or watermark.
